# Advert Assignment Auctions

**Hugo Kai Jun Sun** · Christ's College, University of Cambridge · July 2016 · Essay setter: Dr Frank Kelly

**[Read the essay (PDF, 34 pages)](advert-assignment-auctions.pdf)**

An expository essay on the two auction mechanisms used to sell online advert slots: the Vickrey–Clarke–Groves auction (VCG, used by Facebook) and the Generalised Second Price auction (GSP, used by Google and Bing). It compares their incentives, efficiency and revenue, then studies two extensions: conflation (pooling similar query types into one market) and incomplete information (advertisers who only know the distribution of their rivals' bids).

## Contents

1. **VCG and GSP.** VCG is strategyproof and allocatively efficient; GSP is not truthful, yet both have an efficient Nash equilibrium with the same platform revenue. Both also admit zero-revenue equilibria.
2. **Conflation.** Treating similar queries as one market removes the zero-revenue equilibria and keeps the efficient one. With heterogeneous users, conflated VCG allocates inefficiently but conflated GSP need not (via Milgrom's simplification theorem).
3. **Incomplete information.** In a Bayesian GSP, bid shading means a higher click-through rate on a lower slot can *reduce* platform revenue, and above a threshold no efficient equilibrium exists.
4. **Extensions.** Correlated relevance and position effects, reserve prices, budget-constrained bidding, and convergence dynamics.

Builds on Kelly, Key & Walton, *Efficient Advert Assignment* (2014); Edelman, Ostrovsky & Schwarz (2005); Milgrom (2008); and Gomes & Sweeney (2011).
